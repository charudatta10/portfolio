Research

My doctoral research at NIT Goa investigates hardware acceleration of generative adversarial networks (GANs) on FPGA-based edge devices. It combines evolutionary and multifractal methods, which stabilise training, with hardware-aware design that lowers latency and power.

MFDFA-enhanced GAN

Embeds multifractal detrended fluctuation analysis in the discriminator. Training is more stable and the hardware implementation more efficient, without changing the core GAN architecture.

Latency reduced by 92.05% and power by 86.03%.

Read the paper

Evolutionary-enhanced GAN with wavelet-based discrimination

A genetic-algorithm-optimised generator paired with a wavelet-based discriminator. Synthesised on the AMD Kintex-7 KC705, it has the smallest area footprint among the implementations compared.

Latency of 138 µs against 570 µs, and 24 mW against 47 mW.

Read the paper

2D-MFDFA GAN

A two-dimensional multifractal discriminator for fast synthetic data generation, deployed on the Kintex-7 KC705 through the NNGen framework, with results verified on hardware.

FID improved by 85%, with 94% faster generation and 81% lower power.

Read the paper

About

I am an engineer, researcher and educator working where hardware, machine learning and security meet. My research targets resource-efficient AI for edge systems, my teaching covers incident response and penetration testing, and my software favours small, dependency-light tools. I am especially interested in decentralised technology and open knowledge. Outside work I play chess, run, garden and read.
